# Eight active states after a rational, not dyadic, change

The sixteen-state construction has state maps U=[I,2I] and V=[I;-2I].
Let S=[[I,2I],[2I,I]]. Its determinant is (-3)^8 and

    S^-1 = (-1/3) [[I,-2I],[-2I,I]],
    U S^-1 = [I,0],
    S V = [-3I;0].

Conjugating both orientations on their state banks therefore gives, over Q,

    forward:  Y += J X + R^t z1,
    backward: X -= J Y; z1 += 3 R Y,

while z2 has no cross-input term. The active rectangular factors are
[J,R^t] and [J;-3R]; their product is I. The eight z2 channels are algebraically
passive. All sixteen old state fields have the same full-nine Gaussian and
common scalar endpoint charts, so S commutes with that direct-sum operator.

This does NOT contradict STATE-MINIMUM.md: S is not invertible over Z[1/2].
The old native grammar rejects division by three and non-unit SCALE by three.
No emitted rational native word, admitted passive-helper credit, or full
precision/compiler proof is claimed. The sixteen-state dyadic words remain
the admitted local artifacts. The exact small-matrix identities are checked
in state_minimum.py.

A possible implementation route, still needing integration with the actual
compiler, is fixed-divisor integer-mantissa division. In radix 2^b, a lookup
table indexed by remainder r in {0,1,2} and digit d stores quotient and new
remainder for r*2^b+d. Processing digits high to low is exact long division.
The table has 3*2^b entries and can be generated by additions and a three-state
counter. Rounding to the nearest fixed-point value costs at most half an ulp.
But O(V) work requires a proved bound on the number of mantissa digits per
record and table size relative to V; these cannot be assumed from this sketch.
Scratch ownership, signed values, guard bits and rounding propagation also need
explicit admission. The source's primitive_allowance must not be bypassed.

Even an admitted rational split would leave eight width-nine active states
coupled to width-eight main jobs. It does not construct their global caller,
restoration, independent Gaussian targets or a new kappa.
